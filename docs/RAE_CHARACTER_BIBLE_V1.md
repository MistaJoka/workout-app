## 2. Identity locks

### Character

- **Name:** Rae
- **Age read:** adult woman
- **Character type:** bunny girl
- **Presentation:** warm, confident, supportive, playful, determined
- **Role in app:** guide, workout companion, mascot, progression/reward character
- **Design intent:** recognizable stylized likeness of Rae without photoreal rendering

### Skin

- Warm **medium-deep brown complexion**.
- The final approved reference is the target tonal family.
- Future generations must not lighten Rae relative to the approved v1 reference.
- Use a controlled skin palette ramp for highlight, base, mid-shadow, deep-shadow, and outline.

### Hair

- **Black 4C natural hair.**
- Dense, tightly coiled/kinky texture must be visually legible even in pixel-art simplification.
- Primary silhouette: voluminous natural 4C updo/puff with tightly coiled volume and selected hanging coils/tendrils where composition allows.
- Hair must never be rendered as straight, loosely straightened, silky-straight, or generic wavy hair.
- Animation should preserve the large 4C silhouette; secondary motion is subtle, not flowing like straight hair.

### Face

- Stylized adult likeness, not photorealistic.
- Soft rounded/oval facial structure.
- Friendly expressive eyes.
- Warm smile and readable brows.
- Facial detail should remain compatible with high-bit handheld pixel art.
- Rae does **not** need to look directly at the viewer. Gaze should follow the action, scene, or movement whenever that is more natural.

### Glasses

- Thin **gold round wire-frame glasses**.
- Keep lens geometry and bridge consistent across angles.
- Glasses remain a strong silhouette/identity cue at portrait and sprite scales.

## 3. Bunny anatomy lock

### Ears

This is non-negotiable:

- **Exactly two ears total.**
- Both are bunny ears.
- **No human ears exist or are visible.**
- Outer fur color: **tan**.
- Inner ear color: **flesh pink**.
- Shape: moderately long, semi-upright rabbit ears with a soft natural bend; less floppy than the earliest concepts.
- They emerge from the upper head/crown area and integrate naturally with the 4C hairstyle.
- Ear angle may react to emotion/motion, but ear length, base placement, width, and color remain consistent.

Any asset showing separate human ears is automatically rejected.

### Tail

- One small, round, fluffy **brown bunny tail**.
- Tail is centered anatomically at the back pelvis/upper glute region.
- It should read clearly but remain secondary to Rae's body silhouette.
- No white tail in v1 production assets.

## 4. Body/proportion lock

- Adult fuller/curvy build.
- Strong, athletic visual read without removing her natural curves.
- Grounded human-like proportions adapted to stylized high-bit pixel art.
- Avoid extreme waist reduction, exaggerated chest/hip caricature, or child/teen proportions.
- Exercise frames prioritize believable joint placement and movement clarity over glamour posing.
- Maintain consistent limb lengths, torso length, head scale, shoulder width, hip width, and foot size between assets.

### Logical sprite baseline

- Full-body logical animation canvas: **256 × 256 px**.
- Default high-DPI export: **512 × 512 px at exact 2× nearest-neighbor scale**.
- No non-integer scaling of canonical pixel sources.
- Large reference/hero artwork may exceed this resolution but must not redefine animation proportions.

## 8. Pixel-art style lock

Rae's production art uses **high-bit handheld pixel art** inspired by the density and polish of premium late-GBA/handheld action-RPG artwork without copying any existing franchise character or sprite.

### Required

- intentional pixel clusters;
- crisp silhouette;
- high-detail but controlled color ramps;
- selective color-matched outlines / selout;
- readable forms at small size;
- controlled highlight/shadow placement;
- nearest-neighbor integer scaling for pixel masters;
- stable palette across frames;
- simplified pixel-art facial planes rather than smooth digital-paint skin.

### Reject

- photoreal face rendering;
- painterly airbrush finish on character sprites;
- smooth anime/vector character rendering presented as pixel art;
- random pixel-noise filters;
- excessive anti-aliasing that destroys pixel clusters;
- frame-to-frame palette drift;
- inconsistent anatomy caused by independent AI frame generation.

## 10. View and gaze rules

### Character/marketing/celebration art

- Front, 3/4, side, and back views are allowed.
- Rae may look toward a UI element, another object, the direction of travel, or off-axis.
- Direct eye contact is optional, not the default requirement.

### Exercise instruction art

- Camera is selected for form clarity: usually orthographic side or front.
- Fixed camera, fixed scale, fixed baseline within a movement sequence.
- Rae looks naturally in the movement direction; she should not twist her head toward the viewer simply for engagement.
- Form accuracy outranks personality posing.
